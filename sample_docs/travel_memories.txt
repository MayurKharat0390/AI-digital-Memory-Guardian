Travel Memories & Reflections
==============================

Japan — October 2024
---------------------

The flight from Mumbai to Tokyo took nine hours. I spent most of it reading about the Meiji Restoration, trying to understand how Japan modernized so rapidly without losing its cultural core. The question feels relevant to my own work — how do you adopt new technology (like AI) without eroding what makes you distinctly human?

Tokyo was overwhelming in the best possible way. The first thing that struck me was the silence on the metro. Hundreds of people in a car, and almost no one speaking. It wasn't awkward silence — it was a shared, considerate quiet. I've never felt more conscious of my own phone habits.

We stayed in a ryokan in the Yanaka neighborhood — one of the few areas of Tokyo that survived both the 1923 earthquake and WWII bombing relatively intact. Walking its narrow alleyways felt like slipping through a seam in time. The temple bells in the evening sounded like something I'd heard in a dream.

Kyoto — October 6-9, 2024

Fushimi Inari at 5:30am before the crowds. The endless red torii gates winding up the forested mountain. I climbed all the way to the top — about 4km — and sat at the summit shrine in thick morning mist. There was one other person there, an elderly man raking gravel in the shrine courtyard. We didn't exchange a single word. It was one of the most peaceful hours of my adult life.

The food in Kyoto was quieter than Tokyo's. Less theatrical. My favorite meal was a simple set lunch at a soba restaurant near Nishiki Market: cold soba noodles with dashi broth, a small dish of pickled vegetables, and green tea. The chef had been making soba for 40 years. You could taste the four decades.

Osaka — October 9-11, 2024

Osaka is to Tokyo what Mumbai is to Delhi — louder, messier, more fun, more food-obsessed. Dotonbori at night is pure sensory overload in the best way. I ate takoyaki standing in the street, watched a street performer balance improbable objects, and had the best ramen of my life at a counter with eight seats and a 45-minute queue.

I visited the Osaka Castle on a rainy afternoon. The grounds were mostly empty — everyone seemed to have decided the rain was a reason to stay inside. I disagree. Rain in Japan falls differently. Quieter somehow. I walked the castle moat alone for an hour.

Himachal Pradesh — January 2025
---------------------------------

Drove from Delhi to Manali with my college friend Rohan. We left at 4am to avoid traffic. The first five hours were flat, agricultural, uneventful. Then the mountains appeared — suddenly, definitively — like a wall at the edge of the world.

Rohan and I don't see each other often anymore. We spent those drives catching up on three years of diverged lives. He got married last year. His daughter is eight months old. He showed me videos on his phone and I could see in his eyes the particular exhaustion and wonder that new parents carry.

The Rohtang Pass was closed to civilian vehicles but we found a local jeep driver willing to take us up to a viewpoint just below the snowline. The altitude hit me at around 3,800 meters — a dull headache and a strange, pleasant slowness. The snow-covered peaks in every direction looked airbrushed onto the sky.

We stayed at a small guesthouse in Old Manali run by a family who'd been there for three generations. The grandmother spoke no English but made us the warmest chai I've ever had. Some hospitality needs no language.

The Beas River runs fast and green through the valley. I sat beside it for an hour on our last morning, watching it. Rivers are good at making everything else feel appropriately small.

Reflections on Travel

Every time I travel, I come back with something I can't fully articulate. Not just memories — a recalibration. A reset of what I'm orienting toward. Cities remind me that most of my daily anxiety is optional. Mountains remind me that most of my problems are small.

I've started keeping detailed travel notes because I realized how quickly the texture of a place fades from memory. The smell of that Kyoto soba restaurant, the specific quality of light at Fushimi Inari — these things blur within weeks if not written down. This is exactly why I started building the memory system in the first place.
